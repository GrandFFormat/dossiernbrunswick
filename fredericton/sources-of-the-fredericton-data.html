Sources of the Fredericton data

Here are the sources of the Fredericton data. This site is not the City of Fredericton and has no official status. If anything differs, the City’s own record prevails — it is one click away on every card. These sources feed the decisions of Fredericton City Council and the members’ pages.

Where the data comes from

  • Decisions and meetings come from the minutes of City Council, which the City publishes after each meeting on eSCRIBE, in English (Minutes) and in French (Procès-verbal). Each card links to the PDF of its meeting.
  • Agendas. The City publishes a meeting’s minutes about three months later, once adopted. For meetings that have none yet, we show the agenda, published before the meeting on eSCRIBE: what was put before Council, not what it decided. Those cards are marked “on the agenda” and are replaced when the minutes arrive.
  • Information. The minutes also report what is not voted on: committee reports, presentations, declared conflicts of interest, members’ questions and the order of a special meeting. Those cards are marked “information”: they are not decisions.
  • Council members, their wards and their contact details come from the City’s Mayor and Council page.
  • The City does not publish these documents under an open licence. We have asked its permission to reproduce them here; if it says no, this section will be taken down.

What the minutes do not contain

  • Vote counts. A resolution is “carried” or “defeated”. When the vote is divided (“Carried on division”), the minutes name those who voted against, and so does the card: seven times in 2026. Otherwise they do not say who voted for or against. So the cards never show a full count.
  • Closed sessions. What is discussed there is not in the public minutes. A resolution that comes out of one is then voted on in public; that is the one shown here, marked “From the … Closed Session”.
  • Meetings without a resolution. The special meeting of June 1, 2026, the swearing-in of the new Council, adopted no resolution: it has no card.

What a machine does, and what it can miss

  • Reading the PDFs. A program splits each set of minutes into resolutions, from “Moved by” to the result. A card’s title is the line above the resolution. A few words may come out split from the English PDF (“Cowpe rthwaite”); the City’s PDF prevails.
  • French. The French version is paired with the English one in order. When the two do not have the same number of resolutions (three meetings in 2026: May 10, May 19 and June 8), the meeting stays in English only.
  • Subject tags (zoning, transportation, finance…) are not the City’s: they are a classification added here, by keywords. A wrong tag is our mistake.
  • Absences. A member missing from the list of members present is counted absent for the whole meeting. Leaving partway through is not counted. Absences are counted only for the current Council, sworn in on June 1, 2026: from January to May, it was the previous Council.

Things to know

  • The period. This section covers meetings from January 12 to August 24, 2026, the minutes the City had published as of November 19, 2026. Minutes are adopted at a later meeting, then published: there is always a few weeks’ lag.
  • The PDFs are downloaded by hand. The City’s eSCRIBE site turns robots away. We do not get around that: each set of minutes is downloaded by a person, then read by the program.

What we asked the City, and what it answered
