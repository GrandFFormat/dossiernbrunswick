BILL 12An Act Respecting Young Persons, Digital Markets and Technology UseHis Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the Legislative Assembly of New Brunswick, enacts as follows:PART 1YOUNG CONSUMERS IN DIGITAL MARKETS ACTEnactment of the Young Consumers in Digital Markets Act1 The Young Consumers in Digital Markets Act is enacted as follows: PART 1DEFINITIONS, PURPOSE AND APPLICATIONDefinitions1 The following definitions apply in this Act.“administrative penalty” means a penalty imposed by the issuance of a notice of administrative penalty by an administrative penalty officer under section 25 or by an order of the Tribunal under section 50. (pénalité administrative)“Commission” means the Financial and Consumer Services Commission continued under the Financial and Consumer Services Commission Act. (Commission)“consumer agreement” means an agreement, including any transaction, purchase, agreement to purchase or agreement to comply with terms and conditions, entered into between a provider and a young consumer or the young consumer’s parent or guardian respecting the young consumer’s access to or use of a digital good, digital service or digital platform for personal or family purposes. (convention de consommation)“consumer interaction” means an interaction between a provider and a young consumer respecting the young consumer’s access to or use of a digital good, digital service or digital platform for personal or family purposes and includes an interaction occurring in connection with the advertising, access, provision, operation, promotion, recommendation, display or use of that good, service or platform. (interaction entre fournisseur et consommateur)“Court of King’s Bench” means The Court of King’s Bench of New Brunswick and includes a judge of that court. (Cour du Banc du Roi)“digital good” means data, content, software or any other intangible good in digital form that is available and used electronically, including, but not limited to, an ebook, an audiobook, a downloaded movie, downloaded music, a downloaded game, an application, a virtual item, digital currency and a digital skin. (produit numérique)“digital platform” means a website, application, online service or any other digital system that enables users to access, create, share, view, purchase, exchange, receive, discover or interact with content, goods, services or other users, including, but not limited to, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Snapchat, Discord, YouTube and Roblox, but excludes a website of a business that is used as a storefront for physical products. (plateforme numérique)“digital service” means a service that enables users to access, create, share, view, purchase, exchange, store, process, listen to or interact with digital content or information that is available and used electronically, including, but not limited to, cloud storage, video streaming, music streaming, gaming services, AI services, messaging services and social media services. (service numérique)“Director” means the Director of Digital Markets for Young Consumers appointed under the Financial and Consumer Services Commission Act and includes any person designated by the Commission or the Director to act on the Director’s behalf. (directeur)“material fact” means any information that would reasonably be expected to affect a decision of a young consumer in relation to a consumer agreement or a consumer interaction. (fait important)“Minister” means the Minister of Finance and Treasury Board and includes any person designated by the Minister to act on the Minister’s behalf. (ministre)“provider” means a person who provides, directly or indirectly, a digital good, digital service or digital platform, including an employee or agent of the person but excluding the parent or guardian of a young consumer who is provided with the digital good, digital service or digital platform. (fournisseur)“regulation” means a regulation made under this Act and, unless the context otherwise indicates, includes a rule. (règlement)“regulatory authority” means a person empowered by the laws of a jurisdiction to regulate the activities of a provider. (organisme de réglementation)“rule” means a rule made under this Act or, if the context requires, a rule made under the Financial and Consumer Services Commission Act. (règle)“Small Claims Court” means the Small Claims Court of New Brunswick and includes an adjudicator of that court. (Cour des petites créances)“Tribunal” means the Tribunal defined in the Financial and Consumer Services Commission Act. (Tribunal)“young consumer” means a child under 16 years of age who is a party to a consumer agreement or who participates in a consumer interaction. (jeune consommateur)Purpose2 The purpose of this Act and the regulations is to (a) create conditions that foster the fair treatment of young consumers by regulating consumer agreements and consumer interactions, (b) protect young consumers from unfair, deceptive and exploitative practices in digital markets, and(c) place a duty on providers to ensure protections for young consumers.Application3( 1) This Act and the regulations apply to providers who(a) know or ought reasonably to know that young consumers are using, accessing or interacting with or are likely to be using, accessing or interacting with the digital goods, digital services and digital platforms that the provider is providing, or(b) provide digital goods, digital services or digital platforms that target young consumers.3( 2) This Act or the regulations or any provision of this Act or the regulations does not apply to(a) any person or class of persons exempted from the application of this Act or the regulations or any provision of this Act or the regulations by an order of the Director made under subsection 4(1), (b) any person or class of persons prescribed by regulation, or(c) any agreement or interaction or class of agreement or interaction prescribed by regulation.3( 3) A person exempted under paragraph (2)(b) from the application of this Act or the regulations or any provision of this Act or the regulations shall comply with any terms or conditions prescribed by regulation.Exemptions4( 1) If the Director considers it appropriate to do so, the Director may, by order and subject to any terms and conditions the Director considers appropriate, exempt any person or class of persons from the application of this Act or the regulations or any provision of this Act or the regulations.4( 2) An order under subsection (1) may be made on the Director’s own motion or on the application of an interested person and may be retroactive in its operation.4( 3) An application referred to in subsection (2) shall be on a form provided by the Director and accompanied by the fees prescribed by regulation.4( 4) A person to whom the order applies shall comply with the terms and conditions imposed by the Director under subsection (1).Waiver or release of rights under this Act or the regulations5( 1) No person shall request or require a person to waive or release the person’s rights, benefits or protections under this Act or the regulations except to the extent that the waiver or release is expressly permitted by this Act or the regulations.5( 2) Any waiver or release given by a person of the person’s rights, benefits or protections under this Act or the regulations is void except to the extent that the waiver or release is expressly permitted by this Act or the regulations.5( 3) Any limit imposed on a person’s rights, benefits or protections under this Act or the regulations is void except to the extent that the limit is expressly permitted by this Act or the regulations.Other remedies not precluded6 Any remedy under this Act is in addition to and does not derogate from any other legal, equitable or statutory remedy.PART 2UNFAIR PRACTICESUnfair practices7 It is an unfair practice for a provider to(a) make a representation that may reasonably deceive or mislead a young consumer with respect to a consumer agreement or consumer interaction,(b) exert undue pressure or influence on a young consumer to be a party to a consumer agreement,(c) take advantage of a young consumer’s inability to understand the nature or effect of a consumer agreement, the language in which the consumer agreement is drafted or any other matter in relation to the agreement,(d) fail to disclose a material fact in relation to a consumer agreement,(e) use exaggeration, innuendo or ambiguity concerning a material fact in relation to a consumer agreement,(f) be a party to a consumer agreement if the provider knows or ought to know that the young consumer is unable to receive any reasonable benefit from the digital goods or digital services that the provider offers,(g) include in a consumer agreement terms and conditions that are harsh, oppressive or excessively one sided,(h) make a representation to a young consumer that misrepresents the purpose of any charge or proposed charge,(i) engage in a practice that is misleading, deceptive or exploitative or materially impairs the young consumer’s ability to make an informed decision in relation to a consumer agreement or a consumer interaction,(j) use a design feature, interface, mechanism or practice that has the purpose or effect of encouraging or inducing a young consumer to make a purchase or incur a charge through deception or undue influence,(k) design or operate a digital service or digital platform in a manner that discourages or impedes a young consumer from disengaging from, stopping or pausing its use,(l) use a reward, incentive, scarcity message, countdown, streak, ranking or similar mechanism that creates a false, misleading or unreasonable sense of urgency or scarcity for the purpose of inducing a young consumer to make a purchase or incur a charge,(m) configure default settings for a young consumer that are misleading, deceptive or excessively one sided in favour of the provider,(n) send notifications, prompts or communications to a young consumer during prescribed hours in a manner that encourages continued engagement with a digital good, digital service or digital platform,(o) use personal information, behavioural data or profiling to engage in advertising that targets the young consumer when the advertising is false or misleading or is intended to improperly influence the young consumer’s purchasing decisions, or(p) engage in a practice prescribed by regulation.Prohibition on engaging in an unfair practice8( 1) No person shall engage in an unfair practice.8( 2) In determining whether a person has engaged in an unfair practice, the general impression given by the alleged unfair practice may be considered as well as the literal meaning of the representation or the terms and conditions of the consumer agreement.8( 3) If it is alleged that a provider has engaged in an unfair practice, the onus is on the provider to prove that the provider has not engaged in the unfair practice.Prohibition on advertisements containing unfair practices9( 1) No person shall publish a provider’s advertisement for goods or services if the advertisement contains an unfair practice.9( 2) Subsection (1) does not apply to a person who publishes a provider’s advertisement in good faith and in the ordinary course of business.Circumstances surrounding an unfair practice10( 1) An unfair practice may occur(a) before, after or at the time a consumer agreement is entered into or a consumer interaction takes place,(b) whether or not a consumer agreement is entered into, and(c) whether or not a young consumer or the parent or guardian of the young consumer has suffered loss or damage.10( 2) An unfair practice may consist of a single act or omission.10( 3) An unfair practice is an unfair practice for the purposes of this Act even if(a) it is not directed at a specific young consumer and does not occur in the course of or for the purposes of a specific consumer agreement or consumer interaction but is directed to young consumers generally, and(b) there is no privity of contract between the provider and any specific young consumer affected by the unfair practice.Interpretation11 For the purposes of sections 12 to 17, a young consumer includes the parent or guardian of the young consumer.Cancellation when there has been an unfair practice12( 1) A young consumer may cancel, at no cost or penalty to the young consumer, a consumer agreement to which the young consumer is a party, whether the agreement is documented in writing or oral, if the provider who is a party to the agreement engaged in an unfair practice regarding the consumer agreement or consumer interaction. 12( 2) Subsection (1) applies whether the unfair practice occurred before, after or at the time the consumer agreement was entered into or the consumer interaction took place.12( 3) A young consumer is entitled to recover the amount by which the young consumer’s payment under the consumer agreement exceeds the value of the digital goods or digital services or to recover damages, or both, if cancellation of the consumer agreement is not possible because the return or restitution of the digital goods or cancellation of the digital services is no longer possible.12( 4) When a young consumer cancels a consumer agreement, the cancellation operates to cancel, as if they never existed,(a) the consumer agreement,(b) all related consumer agreements, and(c) all guarantees given in respect of money payable under the consumer agreement.12( 5) The cancellation right is in addition to, and does not affect, any other right or remedy the young consumer has under a consumer agreement or at law.12( 6) If a consumer agreement is cancelled, the provider shall refund to the young consumer all consideration paid by the young consumer under the agreement and any related consumer agreement, whether paid to the provider or another person, within 15 days after the date of cancellation.Notice13( 1) A young consumer who wishes to cancel a consumer agreement under subsection 12(1) or to seek recovery under subsection 12(3) is required to give notice within one year after the consumer agreement is entered into.13( 2) A young consumer may give notice in any manner as long as the notice meets any requirements prescribed by regulation and indicates(a) the young consumer’s intention( i) to cancel the consumer agreement, or( ii) to seek recovery if cancellation is not possible, and(b) the young consumer’s reasons for taking the actions set out in paragraph (a).13( 3) A young consumer may give a notice to a provider by(a) email, (b) personal service, or(c) sending it to the provider by any other method, electronically or otherwise, that permits the young consumer to provide evidence of the cancellation.13( 4) A notice that is given in accordance with paragraph (3)(c) shall be deemed to have been given when sent.13( 5) The young consumer may give the notice to the provider who is a party to the consumer agreement at the address set out in the agreement, or, if the young consumer did not receive a written copy of the agreement, the address of the provider was not set out in the agreement or the agreement was not documented in writing, the young consumer may give the notice(a) at any address of the provider on record with the Commission or the Government of New Brunswick, or(b) at an address of the provider known to the young consumer.13( 6) If notice is given and a satisfactory response is not received within the period of time prescribed by regulation, the young consumer may commence an action in the Court of King’s Bench under section 14.Application to Court of King’s Bench14( 1) A young consumer who has entered into a consumer agreement or participated in a consumer interaction with a provider who has engaged in or consented to an unfair practice in relation to the consumer agreement or consumer interaction may apply to the Court of King’s Bench to commence an action against the provider if the unfair practice has caused the young consumer damage, harm or loss.14( 2) In an action commenced under subsection (1), the Court of King’s Bench may(a) declare that the practice is an unfair practice,(b) award damages,(c) award punitive or exemplary damages,(d) make an order for( i) specific performance with respect to the consumer agreement or consumer interaction,( ii) the recovery of property or funds, or( iii) the cancellation of the consumer agreement,(e) grant an order in the nature of an injunction restraining the provider from engaging in the unfair practice, or(f) give any directions and grant any other relief the Court of King’s Bench considers appropriate.14( 3) In determining whether to grant any relief and the nature and extent of the relief, the Court of King’s Bench shall consider whether the young consumer made a reasonable effort to minimize any damage resulting from the unfair practice and to resolve the dispute with the provider before commencing the action.14( 4) The Court of King’s Bench may award costs in accordance with the Rules of Court.Application to Small Claims Court15 Subject to the jurisdiction of the Small Claims Court, a young consumer may apply to the Small Claims Court to commence an action under subsection 14(1).Powers of the court16( 1) Oral evidence respecting an unfair practice is admissible in an action commenced under section 14 despite the existence of a consumer agreement documented in writing and despite the fact that the oral evidence relates to a representation in respect of a term, condition or undertaking that is not provided for in the consumer agreement.16( 2) The court may disregard the requirement that the young consumer give notice under subsection 13(1) or any requirement relating to the notice if it considers that it is in the interest of justice to do so.Liability17( 1) Each person who engages in an unfair practice is jointly and severally liable, with the provider who is a party to a consumer agreement or who participated in the consumer interaction that was subject to the unfair practice, for any amount to which the young consumer is entitled.17( 2) If a consumer agreement to which section 12 applies has been assigned or if any right to payment under that consumer agreement has been assigned, the liability of the person to whom it has been assigned is limited to the amount paid to that person by the young consumer.17( 3) For greater certainty, for the purposes of this Act, an act or omission of an employee or agent of a person is deemed also to be an act or omission of the person if the act or omission occurred(a) in the course of the employee’s employment with the person, or(b) in the course of the agent’s exercising the powers or performing the duties on behalf of the person under their agency relationship.PART 3RECORD KEEPING AND ADVERTISINGRecord keeping18 A provider shall deliver to the Director or to any other employee of the Commission, at any time that the Director or other employee requires,(a) any of the books, records and documents relating to the business or affairs of the provider with respect to activities governed by this Act or the regulations, and(b) any filings, reports or other communications made to any other regulatory authority.False, misleading or deceptive advertisement19( 1) No person shall make any false, misleading or deceptive statements in any advertisement, circular, pamphlet or similar material prepared or used in respect of an activity governed by this Act or the regulations.19( 2) No person shall publish or otherwise distribute an advertisement, circular, pamphlet or similar material prepared or used in respect of an activity governed by this Act or the regulations that is on the whole misleading even if it does not contain a particular statement that is false, misleading or deceptive.19( 3) If, in the opinion of the Director, a person has made a false, misleading or deceptive statement in any advertisement, circular, pamphlet or similar material, the Director may order the immediate cessation of the use of that material.19( 4) If, in the opinion of the Director, a person has published or otherwise distributed an advertisement, circular, pamphlet or similar material that is on the whole misleading, the Director may order the immediate cessation of the use of that material.19( 5) The Director may order the immediate cessation of the use of any advertisement, circular, pamphlet or similar material that, in the opinion of the Director, contravenes or fails to comply with this Act or the regulations.PART 4COMPLIANCE REVIEWSCompliance review20( 1) The Commission may appoint in writing a person as a compliance officer for the purpose of ensuring compliance with this Act and the regulations.20( 2) The Commission shall issue to every compliance officer a certificate of appointment, and every compliance officer, in carrying out their duties under this Act or the regulations, shall produce their certificate of appointment on request.20( 3) For the purpose of determining whether this Act and the regulations are being complied with, a compliance officer, in carrying out a compliance review, may(a) enter the premises of a provider during normal business hours,(b) require a provider to produce for inspection, examination, auditing or copying any books, records or documents relating to the business or affairs of the provider,(c) inspect, examine, audit or copy the books, records or documents relating to the business or affairs of a provider, and(d) question a provider in relation to the business or affairs of the provider.20( 4) In carrying out a compliance review, a compliance officer may(a) use a data processing system at the premises where the books, records or documents are kept,(b) reproduce any book, record or document, and(c) use any copying equipment at the premises where the books, records or documents are kept to make copies of any book, record or document.20( 5) A compliance officer may carry out a compliance review within or outside the Province.20( 6) A compliance officer shall not enter a private dwelling under subsection (3) unless the compliance officer has the consent of the occupier or has obtained an entry warrant under the Entry Warrants Act.20( 7) Before or after attempting to enter or to have access to any premises, a compliance officer may apply for an entry warrant under the Entry Warrants Act.20( 8) The Commission may, in circumstances prescribed by regulation, require a provider in respect of which a compliance review was carried out to pay the Commission any fee prescribed by regulation and to reimburse the Commission for any expenses prescribed by regulation.Removal of documents21( 1) A compliance officer who removes books, records or documents to make a copy or extract of them or any part of them shall give a receipt to the occupier of the premises for the books, records or documents removed and return the books, records or documents as soon as the circumstances permit after the making of copies or extracts.21( 2) A copy or extract of any book, record or document related to a compliance review and purporting to be certified by a compliance officer is admissible in evidence in any action, proceeding or prosecution and is proof, in the absence of evidence to the contrary, of the original without proof of the appointment, authority or signature of the person purporting to have certified the copy or extract.Misleading statements to compliance officer22 No person shall knowingly make a false or misleading statement, either orally or in writing, to a compliance officer while the compliance officer is engaged in carrying out their duties under this Act or the regulations.Obstruction of compliance officer23( 1) No person shall obstruct or interfere with a compliance officer who is carrying out or attempting to carry out a compliance review or withhold, destroy, conceal, alter or refuse to produce any information or thing reasonably required by a compliance officer for the purposes of the compliance review.23( 2) A refusal of consent to enter a private dwelling is not and shall not be considered to be obstructing or interfering with within the meaning of subsection (1), except if an entry warrant has been obtained.PART 5ADMINISTRATIVE PENALTIESAdministrative penalty officers – appointment 24 The Commission may appoint in writing a person as an administrative penalty officer for the purpose of(a) promoting compliance with this Act and the regulations, or(b) preventing a person from deriving, directly or indirectly, an economic benefit from the contravention of or failure to comply with this Act or the regulations.Administrative penalty by notice25 An administrative penalty officer may impose an administrative penalty by issuing a notice of administrative penalty if the administrative penalty officer(a) determines that a person has contravened or failed to comply with a provision of this Act or the regulations, including( i) a decision, ruling, order, temporary order or direction of the Commission, the Director or the Tribunal made or given under this Act or the regulations, or( ii) a written undertaking made by that person to the Commission, the Director or the Tribunal under this Act or the regulations, and(b) is satisfied that a hearing is not necessary to determine whether the person has contravened or failed to comply with this Act or the regulations.Maximum amount of administrative penalty 26 The amount of an administrative penalty imposed by an administrative penalty officer shall not exceed(a)  $10,000 for an individual, and(b)  $25,000 for a person other than an individual.Determination of amount of administrative penalty 27 In determining the amount of an administrative penalty, an administrative penalty officer shall consider the following matters:(a) whether the contravention of or failure to comply with this Act or the regulations involved an individual or a person other than an individual;(b) the nature and duration of the contravention or failure to comply;(c) the extent to which the person tried to mitigate any loss or take any remedial action;(d) the economic benefit that the person derived or reasonably could have expected to derive from the contravention or failure to comply, directly or indirectly;(e) the extent of the actual or potential harm to others resulting from the contravention or failure to comply;(f) the risk of harm to public confidence in an activity governed by this Act or the regulations resulting from the contravention or failure to comply;(g) whether the person had contravened or failed to comply with a provision of this Act or the regulations within the five-year period before the contravention or failure to comply; and(h) any other matter prescribed by regulation. Notice of administrative penalty 28( 1) A notice of administrative penalty by an administrative penalty officer shall include the following information:(a) the name of the person required to pay the administrative penalty;(b) the provision of this Act or the regulations that the person contravened or failed to comply with and a description of that contravention or failure to comply;(c) the date on which the contravention or failure to comply occurred;(d) the amount of the administrative penalty and the consequences for failing to respond to the notice;(e) the date by which the administrative penalty is required to be paid and how to pay the administrative penalty; (f) a statement that the person may request a review of the administrative penalty in accordance with subsection 32(1); and(g) any other information prescribed by regulation.28( 2) A notice of administrative penalty shall not be issued if the contravention or failure to comply is the subject of a hearing before the Tribunal. 28( 3) A notice of administrative penalty shall not be issued more than one year after the administrative penalty officer first had knowledge of the contravention or failure to comply.Withdrawal of notice of administrative penalty29 An administrative penalty officer may withdraw a notice of administrative penalty if, in the opinion of the administrative penalty officer, the withdrawal of the notice is warranted in the circumstances.Time for paying or disputing administrative penalty30 Subject to section 31, no later than the date set out in a notice of administrative penalty, the person who received the notice shall(a) pay the administrative penalty, or(b) apply for a review of the administrative penalty under section 32.Extension of time for paying administrative penalty31 An administrative penalty officer may extend the time set out in a notice of administrative penalty for paying the administrative penalty if, in the opinion of the administrative penalty officer, the extension is reasonable.Review of administrative penalty32( 1) A person who receives a notice of administrative penalty may request that the Director review the administrative penalty by applying to the Director on a form provided by the Director no later than the date set out in the notice of administrative penalty.32( 2) The Director shall not make a decision with respect to a review without giving the person who received the notice of administrative penalty an opportunity to be heard.32( 3) The Director may confirm or rescind the administrative penalty or may vary the amount of the administrative penalty.32( 4) If the Director confirms the administrative penalty or varies the amount of the administrative penalty, the person who received the notice of administrative penalty shall pay the administrative penalty within 30 days after the Director makes the decision or within a longer period specified by the Director. 32( 5) The decision of the Director under this section is not subject to appeal to the Tribunal.Administrative penalty payable to Commission33 An administrative penalty imposed by an administrative penalty officer is payable to the Commission.Filing notice or decision – Court of King’s Bench34( 1) If a person who is required to pay an administrative penalty under section 30 or subsection 32(4) fails to do so, the amount of the administrative penalty constitutes a debt due to the Commission.34( 2) The Commission may file a certified copy of a notice of administrative penalty or of a decision of the Director under section 32 with a clerk of the Court of King’s Bench, and, on being filed, the notice or decision has the same force and effect as if it were a judgment of the Court of King’s Bench.34( 3) If a notice of administrative penalty or a decision of the Director is filed under subsection (2), the administrative penalty required to be paid to the Commission under the notice or decision may be collected as a judgment of the Court of King’s Bench for the recovery of a debt.Misleading statements to administrative penalty officer35 No person shall knowingly make a false or misleading statement, either orally or in writing, to an administrative penalty officer while the administrative penalty officer is engaged in carrying out the officer’s duties under this Act or the regulations.PART 6INVESTIGATIONSProvision of information to Director36( 1) By an order applicable generally or to one or more persons named or otherwise described in the order, the Director may require a provider or former provider to provide information or to produce books, records or documents or classes of books, records or documents specified or otherwise described in the order within the period or at the intervals specified in the order.36( 2) The Director may make an order under subsection (1)(a) for the administration of this Act or the regulations, or(b) to assist in the administration of similar legislation of another jurisdiction.36( 3) The Director may require that the authenticity, accuracy or completeness of information provided or of a book, record or document or a class of books, records or documents produced under an order under subsection (1) be verified by affidavit.36( 4) The Director may require that the information provided or that the books, records or documents produced be delivered in electronic form if the information or the books, records or documents are already available in that form.Investigation order37( 1) The Commission may make an order appointing a person as an investigator to conduct any investigation that the Commission considers expedient(a) for the administration of this Act or the regulations, or(b) to assist in the administration of similar legislation of another jurisdiction.37( 2) In its order, the Commission shall specify the scope of the investigation to be carried out.Powers of investigator38( 1) An investigator may, with respect to the person who is the subject of the investigation, investigate, inspect and examine(a) the business or affairs of that person, (b) any books, records, documents or communications connected with that person, and (c) any property or assets owned, acquired or disposed of, in whole or in part, by that person or by a person acting on behalf of or as agent for that person.38( 2) For the purposes of an investigation under this Part, an investigator may inspect and examine any book, record, document or thing, whether in possession or control of the person in respect of whom the investigation is ordered or any other person.38( 3) An investigator conducting an investigation under this Part may, on production of the order appointing them,(a) enter the business premises of any person named in the order during normal business hours and inspect and examine any book, record, document or thing that is used in the business of that person and that relates to the order,(b) require the production of any book, record, document or thing referred to in paragraph (a) for inspection or examination, and(c) on giving a receipt, remove the book, record, document or thing inspected or examined under paragraph (a) or (b) for the purpose of further inspection or examination.38( 4) Inspection or examination under this section shall be completed as soon as the circumstances permit, and the books, records, documents or things shall be returned promptly to the person who produced them.38( 5) No person shall withhold, destroy, conceal, alter or refuse to give any information or withhold, destroy, conceal, alter or refuse to produce any book, record, document or thing reasonably required under subsection (3) by an investigator.Power to compel evidence39( 1) An investigator conducting an investigation under this Part has the same power to summon and enforce the attendance of witnesses, to compel witnesses to give evidence under oath or in any other manner and to compel witnesses to produce books, records, documents and things or classes of books, records, documents and things as the Court of King’s Bench has for the trial of civil actions.39( 2) On the application of an investigator to the Court of King’s Bench, the failure or refusal of a person to attend, to take an oath, to answer questions or to produce books, records, documents and things or classes of books, records, documents and things in the custody, possession or control of the person makes the person liable to be committed for contempt as if in breach of an order or judgment of the court.39( 3) A person giving evidence at an investigation may be represented by legal counsel.39( 4) Testimony given by a person under this section shall not be admitted into evidence against that person in any prosecution other than for perjury in the giving of that testimony or the giving of evidence contradictory to that testimony.Investigators authorized as peace officers40 Every investigator in carrying out their duties under this Act and the regulations is a person employed for the preservation and maintenance of the public peace and has and may exercise all the powers, authorities and immunities of a peace officer as defined in the Criminal Code (Canada).Seized property41( 1) On request to the investigator by the person who, at the time of the seizure, was in lawful possession of books, records, documents or things seized under this Part, the books, records, documents or things seized shall, at a time and place mutually convenient to that person and the investigator, be made available for consultation and copying by that person.41( 2) If property is seized and the matter for which the property was seized is concluded, the investigator shall return the property to the person who was in lawful possession of it at the time of the seizure within 60 days after the day that the matter is concluded.41( 3) If property is seized and the person who was in lawful possession of the property at the time of the seizure alleges that the property is not relevant in respect of the matter for which it was seized, the person may apply by notice of motion to the Court of King’s Bench for the return of the property.41( 4) On a motion under subsection (3), the Court of King’s Bench shall order the return of any property that it determines is not relevant to the matter for which it was seized to the person who was in lawful possession of the property at the time of the seizure.Report of investigation42( 1) At the request of the Commission, an investigator who made an investigation shall provide a report of the investigation to the Commission or any transcripts of evidence or any material or other things in the investigator’s possession relating to the investigation.42( 2) A report that is provided to the Commission is privileged and is inadmissible in evidence in any action or other proceeding.Prohibition against disclosure43( 1) For the purpose of protecting the integrity of an investigation, the Commission may make an order that applies for the duration of the investigation prohibiting a person from disclosing to any person other than the person’s lawyer the following information: (a) the fact that an investigation is being conducted;(b) the name of any person examined or sought to be examined;(c) the nature or content of any questions asked;(d) the nature or content of any demands for the production of any document or other thing; or (e) the fact that any document or other thing was produced.43( 2) An order under subsection (1) does not apply to disclosures authorized by the
