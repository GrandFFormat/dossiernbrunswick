BILL 3An Act Respecting the Child, Youth, Senior and Mental Health AdvocateHis Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the Legislative Assembly of New Brunswick, enacts as follows:Child, Youth and Senior Advocate Act1( 1) The title of the Child, Youth and Senior Advocate Act, chapter C-2.7 of the Acts of New Brunswick, 2007, is repealed and the following is substituted:Child, Youth, Senior and Mental Health Advocate Act1( 2) Section 1 of the Act is amended(a) in the definition “Advocate” by striking out “Child, Youth and Senior Advocate” and substituting “Child, Youth, Senior and Mental Health Advocate”;(b) in the definition “service” by striking out “and seniors” wherever it appears and substituting “, seniors and persons living with mental health challenges”;(c) by adding the following definitions in alphabetical order: “person living with mental health challenges” means a person of any age who is experiencing a cognitive, emotional or behavioural disturbance, including a mental disorder described in the most recent edition of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders published by the American Psychiatric Association. (personne vivant avec des difficultés de santé mentale)“young adult” means a person who is 19 years of age or older, but under the age of 26 years, and who, on the person’s nineteenth birthday, is eligible for social services under section 29 or 31 of the Child and Youth Well-Being Act. Mental Health Advocate1( 4) Section 2 of the Act is repealed and the following is substituted: Office of the Child, Youth, Senior and Mental Health Advocate2 There is established the Office of the Child, Youth, Senior and Mental Health Advocate, which office is charged with the following duties and responsibilities with respect to children, youths, adults under protection, seniors and persons living with mental health challenges:(a) ensuring that their rights and interests are protected;(b) ensuring that their views are heard and considered in appropriate forums where those views might not otherwise be advanced;(c) ensuring that they have access to services and that their complaints about those services receive appropriate attention;(d) providing information and advice to the government, government agencies and communities about the availability, effectiveness, responsiveness and relevance of services provided to them; and(e) acting generally as an advocate for their rights and interests.1( 5) Section 3 of the Act is amended(a) in subsection (1) by striking out “Child, Youth and Senior Advocate” and substituting “Child, Youth, Senior and Mental Health
