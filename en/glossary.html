The jargon, in plain words

The glossary

The words the Assembly uses, explained simply. Nothing here is official: it is a reading aid.

First reading
The tabling. The bill is introduced and printed. It is not debated yet, and nobody votes on its content.
Second reading
The debate on the principle. The House discusses the idea of the bill, not yet its clauses. This is the stage where a bill can be defeated outright.
Committee
Clause-by-clause study by a committee of members. This is where the text can be amended. In New Brunswick a private bill goes to committee before its second reading: the order is not always the same.
Third reading
The House's final vote. After it, the text no longer changes.
Royal assent
The Lieutenant-Governor's signature, which turns a bill into an Act. Careful: an Act that has received assent is not necessarily in force — many wait on a proclamation, sometimes months later.
Government bill
A bill introduced by a minister, on behalf of the government. These are the vast majority of bills that pass.
Private member's public bill
A bill introduced by a member who is not a minister. It rarely becomes law.
Private bill
A bill aimed at one person, company or body — amending a university's founding Act, for instance, or a professional association's.
Recorded division
A vote where every member's name is entered in the Journal. They are rare: most bills pass on a voice vote, with no individual positions recorded. No recorded vote therefore does not mean "unanimous".
Journal
The official record of the decisions of the House, published each sitting day. It is the only place New Brunswick's recorded votes appear.
Hansard
The word-for-word record of debate. It appears several months after the sitting.
Order paper
The agenda of the House: what is before it, and in what order.
Petition
In New Brunswick a petition is on paper, signed by hand, and can only be presented by a member. There is no online petition system. A minister must reply in writing within two weeks, but that reply is not published.
Speaker
The member who presides over sittings. The Speaker does not vote: a recorded division therefore never exceeds 48 of the 49 seats.
Session
A period of business. Every new session renumbers bills from 1 — the same number means different bills in different sessions.
Vote attendance
The share of the session's recorded divisions in which a person's name is entered in the Journal, yea or nay. It is not an official measure: the Assembly publishes none, and this one is worked out here. It says little while a session holds few recorded divisions — and since they are rare in New Brunswick, that is often the case. "n/a" means no recorded division has this person on file: that is missing information, not an absence. The Speaker never votes.
