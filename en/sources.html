What changed on DossierNBrunswick, newest first
← Back to overview
Hi! My name is Martin Archambault I'm 45, and dossiernbrunswick.ca is your first step into democracy!

If we want people to understand politics, it has to be brought down to their level.

When I first tried to take an interest in it, on top of not knowing the jargon, bills came as two lines… and fifty PDFs. Most people would simply walk away.

So I had an idea: use artificial intelligence first to read through all of it, then to put it in plain words. That's how DossierQuébec was born — and now DossierNBrunswick.

Alongside the AI recap, I've given people a lexicon that explains the basics of political jargon. Plain-language law exists — think of this as its equivalent for politics!

    • November 8, 2026 — Full English version Every page now has its own English version, under /en/: bills, ministers, votes, the glossary. Each bill has its own title and links to the other bills of its session; the sponsor links to their profile.
    • November 8, 2026 — Link to DossierCanada The maple leaf is back in the header, right of the name. One click opens DossierCanada, the federal site.
    • November 8, 2026 — Clearer home page The site’s mission is highlighted under the title: by person, in plain language, no judgment, no verdict. In dark mode, flagged bills are easier to read.
    • November 8, 2026 — DossierNBrunswick opens The bills of the 61st Legislature, their stages and the date of each reading, the recorded divisions read in the Assembly's Journals, the Executive Council and the 49 MLAs, drawn from legnb.ca in both official languages.
